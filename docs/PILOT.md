# UAE restaurant pilot conversation

## Opening pitch

“Turn each table into an ordering point. Guests order from their phones, your team controls preparation, and you reduce repetitive order-taking work.”

ChestBest is a fictional demonstration. Do not promise headcount or payroll reductions before measuring real service.

## Five-minute walkthrough

1. Show table 1’s QR and guest menu. Switch to Arabic to demonstrate RTL support.
2. Submit two burgers, hotshots and a drink. Show automatic appearance on a separate kitchen screen.
3. Advance preparation and show the guest status update. Explain that queued means received, not cooking.
4. Place an additional ticket and request staff assistance.
5. Serve the tickets, record a simulated payment, close the visit, and show the fresh guest session.
6. Toggle a sold-out item and edit menu pricing from the owner view.

## Questions before proposing a live pilot

- Where does peak-time delay happen: taking orders, entering the till, kitchen preparation, or payment?
- How many minutes per shift are spent taking and re-entering dine-in orders?
- Which POS, kitchen printer/display, and payment terminals are already in use?
- Who monitors the front desk, and what happens when an incoming order goes unnoticed?
- How are sold-out items, mistakes, refunds, table moves and dietary requests handled?
- Would customers prefer delivery to the table or collection at a counter?
- What would make a monthly subscription worthwhile, and who approves it?

## Pilot measurement

Agree the same busy service periods before and during a pilot. Record ready-to-order to accepted-order time, staff minutes per order, re-entry work, corrections, guest adoption, additional orders, and missed tickets. Separate order-taking time from cooking time. Compare support and software costs with observed savings; record whether the owner commits to paying after the pilot.

Test fixed monthly pricing per location after learning the workflow and support cost. No price is presented as validated. Require an agreed POS/billing flow, staff access controls, fake-order protection, connectivity fallback, and named service owner before taking real orders.
